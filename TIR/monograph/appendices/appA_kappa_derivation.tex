\paragraph{Established context.}
The informational and thermodynamic vocabulary surrounding $\ln2$ is grounded
in Shannon, Landauer, Bennett, and modern information theory
\cite{shannon1948,Landauer1961,Bennett1982,CoverThomas2006}.  Geometric-phase
normalization is compared with Simon and Berry \cite{Simon1983,berry1984}.  The
coefficient $\ln2/(24\pi)$ is a Metatime/TIR structural definition, not a
standard invariant established by those sources.
% CITATION_CONTEXT_V10_9_END

\claimstatus{B -- model postulate with structural motivation.}

\section{Established ingredients}
A binary equiprobable choice has Shannon entropy $\ln2$ in natural units
\cite{shannon1948}.  A cyclic quantum evolution may acquire a geometric phase
determined by a connection and its holonomy \cite{Simon1983,berry1984}.  Pure
states in a three-dimensional complex Hilbert space form $\mathbb{C}P^2$, not
$\mathbb{C}P^3$; the normalization of any Fubini--Study or symplectic volume must
be specified before assigning it a numerical value.

\section{Metatime normalization postulate}
The framework defines
\[
  \boxed{\kappa\equiv\frac{\ln2}{24\pi}}
  =0.00919315000636\ldots .
\]
The original discrete normalization was written
\[
  24\pi=2\,|A_4|\,\pi,
  \qquad |A_4|=12,
\]
combining a two-orientation factor, the order of the rotational tetrahedral
group, and a reference half-turn phase scale.  This is an ansatz internal to
the framework.  It is not asserted to be the symplectic volume of
$\mathbb{C}P^3$, a theorem about the Standard Model, or a Coxeter invariant of
$A_4$.  Standard Coxeter-group terminology should not be conflated with the
alternating group $A_4$ \cite{Humphreys1990}.

\section{Normalized-cycle factorization}
The information-spinor crosswalk in
Appendix~\ref{app:information_spinor_crosswalk} separates recurrence from its
radian representation.  Let $q\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ denote normalized turns
and let $C$ denote the angular closure period.  If the model assigns
\[
\frac{\dd I}{\dd q}=\frac{\ln2}{12},
\]
then the angular information density is exactly
\[
\frac{\dd I}{\dd\phi}=\frac{\ln2}{12C}.
\]
Choosing the standard radian representation $C=2\pi$ reproduces
\[
\frac{\dd I}{\dd\phi}=\frac{\ln2}{24\pi}=\kappa.
\]
This is a useful conditional cross-derivation because the arithmetic cycle
assignment and the geometric closure are exposed separately.  It does not by
itself derive the twelve-cycle assignment from established physics.

\section{Cross-factorizations of the integer 24}
The identity
\[
24=8\times3=12\times2=6\times4
\]
is exact arithmetic.  Metatime/TIR may use these factorizations as mutually
compatible semantic decompositions---for example declared mixing sectors and
flavours, projective cycles and half-turns, or spinor cycles and lifted
half-turns.  Their numerical agreement is an internal consistency constraint;
it is not three statistically independent experimental derivations of the same
physical constant.

Likewise, appearances of the integer $24$ in modular forms, lattices, group
orders, or unrelated counting problems do not establish a physical connection
without an explicit map and theorem.  The solver introduced in the dedicated
critical-axis programme therefore labels the arithmetic equalities separately
from their model-level physical interpretations.

\section{Parameter-counting statement}
The numerical value of $\kappa$ is not obtained by a continuous fit.  It does,
however, depend on discrete structural choices, including the model's cycle and
sector assignments.  The appropriate publication claim is therefore ``no
continuously fitted coefficient in the definition,'' not ``derived uniquely
from first principles.''  Its physical relevance must be tested by transfer to
observables and by prospective predictions.
