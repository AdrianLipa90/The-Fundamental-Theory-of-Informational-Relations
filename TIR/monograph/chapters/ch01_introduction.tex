\section{Motivation}
The Standard Model contains measured masses, couplings, and mixing quantities
whose numerical values motivate a search for lower-dimensional relational
structure behind the observed hierarchy. TIR develops such a programme from
binary information, projective quantum geometry, relational carriers, and
flavour mixing.

\section{Aim and scope}
This monograph investigates whether a compact set of information-geometric and
relational structures can organize particle, flavour, hadronic, cosmological,
and geometric observables. The publication architecture separates exact
mathematical identities, TIR structural derivations, sector assignments,
diagnostics, empirical tests, and prospectively frozen predictions.

The standard geometric background is the projective space of quantum rays,
equipped with the Fubini--Study metric and Berry curvature
\cite{ProvostVallee1980,Simon1983,berry1984}. For a normalized state
$|\psi\rangle$, the line element may be written
\begin{equation}
  ds_{\rm FS}^2=\langle d\psi|d\psi\rangle
  -|\langle\psi|d\psi\rangle|^2.
  \label{eq:fubini_study}
\end{equation}

\section{Primitive information and the $\kappa$ normalization}

\[
\sigma_\star=\frac12,
\qquad

\]

\[

\[
SU(3)_F.
\]
Therefore
\[
\dim\mathfrak{su}(3)_F=3^2-1=8.
\]
Eight independent mixing directions across three flavours give
\[
\boxed{N_{\rm mix}=8\cdot3=24.}
\]
The primitive half coordinate supplies the half-turn phase
\[
\Delta\phi_{1/2}=\frac12(2\pi)=\pi,
\]

\[
\boxed{\Phi_{\rm mix}=24\pi.}
\]

\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
  \kappa
  =\frac{H_{\rm bin}(1/2)}{\Phi_{\rm mix}}
  =\frac{\ln2}{3(3^2-1)\pi}
  =\frac{\ln2}{24\pi}.}
  \label{eq:kappa_def}
\end{equation}
