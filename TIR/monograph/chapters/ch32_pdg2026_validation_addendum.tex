\section{Status}
This chapter records the first TIR/Metatime cross-check against the 2026
Review of Particle Physics.  It supersedes older numerical validation language
where the older frozen comparison conflicts with the 2026 audit.  No coefficient
is changed here to improve agreement.

\section{Validation semantics}
A small relative percentage error is not equivalent to precision agreement.
The audit separates numerical compatibility, tension, precision failure,
provenance-contaminated postdiction, internal formula failure, and quantities
that are not yet sufficiently defined for precision testing.

\section{Principal corrections}
The previous statement that all displayed PMNS parameters lie within
$1.6\sigma$ is withdrawn: the frozen $\sin^2\theta_{13}=1/49$ is roughly
$2.7$--$4.0\sigma$ below the selected 2026 global-fit best values.  The old
near-match of $\delta_{CP}$ to $244^\circ$ is likewise not a current-best-fit
validation statement.

The printed pion and kaon expressions are quarantined.  Literal evaluation of
$E_Pe^{-6/\pi}$ and $E_Pe^{-(\zeta(2)-1)}$ does not produce the printed meson
masses; an independently derived absolute action baseline is required before
those equations can be treated as predictions.

The phrase ``essentially perfect match'' for the fine-structure constant is
withdrawn as a precision claim.  The relative discrepancy is small, but the
uncertainty of $\alpha^{-1}(0)$ is vastly smaller.

The current $M_W$ and $M_Z$ relations are precision failures.  The Higgs
relation $M_H=126.07$ GeV is reopened as a precision tension; the earlier claim
that it was within $2\sigma$ of $125.25\pm0.17$ GeV is arithmetically false.

\section{Formula-freeze rule}
Any future TIR claim intended as prospective evidence shall publish before
unblinding: the formula/version hash, external inputs, construction data,
numerical prediction, and decision rule.  FAIL and TENSION results remain in
the public validation ledger and may motivate a new version, but may not be
silently overwritten.
