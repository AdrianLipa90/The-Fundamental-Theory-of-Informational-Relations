\section{The Information Constant \(\kappa\)}

The Metatime framework defines the information constant
\begin{equation}
\kappa \equiv \frac{\ln 2}{24\pi}.
\label{eq:kappa_framework_definition}
\end{equation}
It is a model postulate with structural motivation, not a first-principles
consequence of standard quantum mechanics, information geometry, or the
Standard Model.  Appendix~\ref{app:kappa} records the normalization assumptions,
the exact phase-rate consequences of the definition, and the corresponding
parameter-counting statement.

\claimstatus{B -- model postulate with structural motivation.}

\subsection{Geometric-phase reference scale}

Consider a two-level quantum system (a spin-$1/2$ particle) whose Hamiltonian
depends adiabatically on parameters $\mathbf{R}(t)$.  After the parameters
complete a closed cycle in parameter space, the state may acquire a geometric
phase~\cite{berry1984}:

\begin{equation}
\gamma_n = i \oint \langle n(\mathbf{R}) | \nabla_{\mathbf{R}} n(\mathbf{R}) \rangle \cdot d\mathbf{R}.
\label{eq:berry_general}
\end{equation}

For a spin-$1/2$ eigenstate on the Bloch sphere, the Berry phase is, up to
orientation convention,
\begin{equation}
\gamma = -\frac{\Omega}{2}\pmod{2\pi},
\label{eq:berry_spin_half}
\end{equation}
where $\Omega$ is the enclosed solid angle.  A loop enclosing a hemisphere
($|\Omega|=2\pi$) therefore gives a phase of magnitude $\pi$ modulo $2\pi$.
A full-sphere solid angle $4\pi$ instead gives magnitude $2\pi$, which is
trivial modulo $2\pi$; it does \emph{not} produce $\pi$.

Accordingly, the factor $\pi$ used in the Metatime normalization is treated as
a chosen spinorial/geometric reference phase scale.  The standard Berry formula
motivates the availability of this phase scale but does not uniquely derive the
coefficient $1/(24\pi)$.

\subsection{Binary Shannon information}

For a binary equiprobable distribution, Shannon entropy is~\cite{shannon1948}
\begin{equation}
H\!\left(\frac12,\frac12\right)=\ln2
\quad\text{(nats)}.
\label{eq:shannon_binary}
\end{equation}
This is an exact information-theoretic statement.  Its use as the numerator of
$\kappa$ is part of the Metatime structural ansatz.

\subsection{Tetrahedral normalization}

The rotational symmetry group of a regular tetrahedron is $A_4$ and has order
12.  The full tetrahedral symmetry group including reflections is isomorphic to
$S_4$ and has order 24.  In the present normalization the integer 24 is written
as
\begin{equation}
24 = 2\,|A_4|,
\qquad |A_4|=12,
\label{eq:tetrahedral_normalization}
\end{equation}
where the additional factor two is an explicit orientation normalization.
This discrete choice is internal to TIR; it is not identified with the Weyl
group of $SU(3)$, which is $S_3$ of order 6.

\subsection{Structural normalization of \(\kappa\)}

Combining the binary information scale, the declared tetrahedral-orientation
normalization, and the reference phase scale gives the TIR definition

\begin{equation}
\boxed{\kappa \equiv \frac{\ln 2}{2|A_4|\pi}
=\frac{\ln 2}{24\pi}}.
\label{eq:kappa_construction}
\end{equation}

\noindent Evaluating numerically:

\[
\kappa = \frac{0.6931471805599453}{24 \times 3.141592653589793}
       = 0.009193150006360484\ldots .
\]

\noindent The numerical value is not continuously fitted, but it does depend on
the declared discrete normalization choices.  Therefore the correct status is
``structurally defined and prospectively testable,'' not ``uniquely derived from
first principles.''

\section{The Exponential Mass Formula}

A central Metatime ansatz for selected mass relations is

\begin{equation}
m = E_P \, e^{-S/\kappa}.
\label{eq:mass_formula}
\end{equation}

Here $E_P = \sqrt{\hbar c^5 / G} = 1.22089 \times 10^{22}$~MeV is the Planck
energy, and $S$ is a dimensionless action constructed from the declared
L-constants, arithmetic assignments and sector-specific rules.  Equation
\eqref{eq:mass_formula} is a model law to be tested against the programme's
prospective observables; it is not asserted as an established universal law for
every physical mass.

\claimstatus{Model ansatz; physical validity determined sector by sector.}

\subsection{Why the Exponential Form?}

The exponential form is motivated by comparison with statistical weights such
as
\[
Z = \sum_i e^{-E_i / k_B T}.
\]
In the Metatime construction, $\kappa$ sets a dimensionless information-phase
scale and $S$ is used as an action-like coordinate.  This analogy motivates
$\exp(-S/\kappa)$ but does not derive it from equilibrium statistical mechanics.
Consequently, agreement or failure of the resulting mass relations must be
tracked empirically rather than inferred from the analogy itself.

\section{Action Construction Principles}

The current model builds sector actions $S$ from combinations of the following
elements:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Spin-labelled terms:} terms involving $1/2$ in fermionic sector
assignments.
\item \textbf{Generation terms:} terms proportional to $\kappa$ in the declared
generation-release constructions.
\item \textbf{Flavour terms:} terms involving quark-prime assignments and
L-constants $L_3,L_4,L_5$.
\item \textbf{Geometric corrections:} ratios or powers of declared geometric
quantities and $\kappa$ used by specific candidate relations.
\end{enumerate}

These are model-building rules.  Their presence in a formula does not by itself
establish a microscopic physical mechanism, and retrospective assignments are
kept distinct from prospective tests elsewhere in the monograph.

\section{The NOEMA Tetrahedron}

The NOEMA tetrahedron is a model-space construction in the operator coordinates

\[
\{\hat I,\ \hat M,\ \hat A\},
\]

where $\hat I$, $\hat M$, and $\hat A$ are the isospin-, mass-, and axial-labelled
coordinates used by the framework.  TIR uses the tetrahedral picture to organize
candidate relations among flavour and mixing quantities.  Statements that edge
lengths, face areas, or dihedral angles correspond to physical observables are
therefore model assignments to be audited against the CKM and PMNS sectors, not
established consequences of tetrahedral geometry alone.

absolute scale in the exponential mass ansatz.
\item[$E_{\text{proton}} = 938.272$~MeV:] the proton mass, used as a reference
scale in selected baryonic and electroweak constructions.
\item[$N_c = 3$:] the number of QCD colours in the anomaly-sector bookkeeping.
\item[$Y(L) = -1/2$:] the lepton-doublet hypercharge used in the anomaly checks.
\item[Strong-CP conversion input:] the numerical conversion used to translate
the model $\theta_{\rm QCD}$ relation into the neutron-EDM observable.

The remaining numerical relations are generated from the declared TIR
constants, discrete assignments and sector rules.  This should not be read as a
claim that all such relations are independent predictions: the publication
protocol explicitly separates anchors, retrospective assignments, prospective
predictions, upper-limit tests and physical failures.
