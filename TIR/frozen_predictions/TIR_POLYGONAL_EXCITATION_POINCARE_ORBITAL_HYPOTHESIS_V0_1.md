## Frozen hypothesis

The current TIR geometric construction uses tetrahedral mappings in which a pole is related to a three-point base. The hypothesis is that the three-point base is the lowest member of a broader excitation hierarchy rather than the only admissible base geometry.

Let the base cardinality be

\[
N \ge 3.
\]

The frozen proposal is:

\[
N=3 \rightarrow \text{triangular base / tetrahedral ground geometry},
\]

while excitation may permit

\[
N=4,5,6,\ldots
\]

with the pole connected to an \(N\)-point polygonal base. In particular,

\[
N=4
\]

corresponds to a square base viewed from the pole.

The excitation ladder is therefore proposed as

\[
B_3 \rightarrow B_4 \rightarrow B_5 \rightarrow \cdots,
\]

with no finite upper level assumed at freeze time.

## Poincare-disk prediction

The same polygonal excitation hierarchy is predicted to admit a projection onto the Poincare disk in which successive polygonal base states generate a hierarchy of orbital-like geometric states around the projected pole/center.

A minimal frozen representation of an \(N\)-point base is

\[
z_k^{(N)} = r_N \exp\!\left[i\left(\phi_N+\frac{2\pi k}{N}\right)\right],
\qquad k=0,\ldots,N-1.
\]

The prediction to be tested later is that the hierarchy

\[
N=3,4,5,\ldots
\]

can generate discrete excitation structure on the Poincare disk and may map onto atomic-orbital structure.

## Frozen physical claim

The bold hypothesis is that atomic orbital levels may arise from, or be representable by, successive polygonal excitation states of the same pole-to-base geometry used in TIR, with the tetrahedral \(N=3\) construction acting as the lowest member of an excitation family.

This statement is frozen as a prediction. It has not yet been mathematically verified.
