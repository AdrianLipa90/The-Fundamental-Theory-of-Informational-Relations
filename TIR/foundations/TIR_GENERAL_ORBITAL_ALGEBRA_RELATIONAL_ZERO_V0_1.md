## 1. Relational starting point

TIR uses relational existence typing:

\[
\operatorname{Exist}(x)\Longrightarrow \exists R_x.
\]

Absolute nothing is defined by absence of relation, distinction and referent,

\[
\mathsf N := \neg\exists R.
\]

Therefore an existing absolute nothing would satisfy both

\[
\operatorname{Exist}(\mathsf N)\Longrightarrow \exists R_{\mathsf N}
\]

and

\[
\mathsf N\Longrightarrow\neg\exists R,
\]

hence it is non-realizable within this typing:

\[
\boxed{\neg\operatorname{Exist}(\mathsf N)}.
\]

Arithmetic zero is not identified with \(\mathsf N\). Zero is a typed null value of an admitted relation or map.

## 2. Minimal dual relation and the relational midpoint

Let a nontrivial ordered relation have distinct endpoint roles \(a\neq b\). Orientation exchange is the involution

\[
\mathcal J(a)=b,\qquad \mathcal J(b)=a,\qquad \mathcal J^2=\mathrm{id}.
\]

On the affine carrier between the endpoint coordinates, the exchange map is

\[
\mathcal J(x)=a+b-x.
\]

A fixed point \(m\) satisfies

\[
m=\mathcal J(m)=a+b-m,
\]

therefore

\[
\boxed{m=\frac{a+b}{2}}.
\]

This midpoint is unique.

Normalize the endpoints by

\[
q(x)=\frac{x-a}{b-a},
\]

so that

\[
q(a)=0,\qquad q(b)=1.
\]

Then exchange acts as

\[
q\mapsto1-q
\]

and its unique fixed point is

\[
\boxed{q_*=\frac12}.
\]

Define the centered relational coordinate

\[
\boxed{Z=2q-1}.
\]

Then

\[
q=0\mapsto Z=-1,\qquad
q=\frac12\mapsto Z=0,\qquad
q=1\mapsto Z=+1,
\]

and exchange becomes

\[
\boxed{Z\mapsto-Z}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{\frac12_{[0,1]}=0_R}
\]

where \(0_R\) denotes the zero of the centered relational coordinate, not arithmetic identity with the number \(0\).

### Theorem 2.1 — Relational Zero Theorem

For every nondegenerate affine two-endpoint relation with endpoint exchange involution, the unique exchange-invariant point is the midpoint. Under normalization to \([0,1]\), that point has coordinate \(1/2\); under centered relational coordinates it is \(0_R\).

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Fix}(\mathcal J)
=
\left\{\frac{a+b}{2}\right\}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
q_*=\frac12
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
Z_*=0_R.
}
\]

No Bloch sphere, Hilbert space, Berry connection or spin representation is needed for this result.

## 3. Orbital twist and the complex structure

The relational midpoint supplies the center. Orbital structure requires a reversible oriented twist about that center.

Let \(V\) be the minimal real orbital plane around \(0_R\), and let \(K:V\to V\) be the orientation generator satisfying

\[
\boxed{K^2=-I}.
\]

The real algebra generated by \(I\) and \(K\),

\[
\{aI+bK:a,b\in\mathbb R\},
\]

is isomorphic to the complex numbers under

\[
I\leftrightarrow1,\qquad K\leftrightarrow i.
\]

Thus the complex carrier is the canonical representation of an oriented two-dimensional orbital plane with a quarter-turn operator:

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{I,K\}
\cong\mathbb C.
}
\]

This statement does not claim that a bare relation alone forces a complex structure. The required additional structure is the reversible oriented orbital twist \(K^2=-I\). Once such a twist is admitted, the complex representation is forced up to isomorphism.

## 4. \(U(1)\) as orbital symmetry around relational zero

Exponentiating the twist generator gives the one-parameter action

\[
R(\phi)=e^{\phi K}
=
\cos\phi\,I+\sin\phi\,K.
\]

Because \(K\) is the complex quarter-turn,

\[
R(\phi)\leftrightarrow e^{i\phi}.
\]

The norm-preserving continuous orbit through any \(v\neq0_R\) is

\[
\mathcal O_v
=
\{R(\phi)v:\phi\in\mathbb R\}
\simeq S^1.
\]

The transformation group is

\[
\boxed{
SO(2)\cong U(1)\cong S^1.
}
\]

The relational zero is fixed by the full action:

\[
R(\phi)\,0_R=0_R
\qquad
\forall\phi.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
0_R=\operatorname{Fix}(U(1))
}
\]

for the minimal orbital plane, while each nonzero constant-radius state traces a \(U(1)\) orbit around it.

### Theorem 4.1 — Orbital Phase Theorem

Given:

1. a centered relational zero \(0_R\);
2. a two-dimensional real orbital carrier \(V\);
3. a reversible orientation operator \(K\) with \(K^2=-I\);
4. preservation of the quadratic radius \(r^2=\langle v,v\rangle\);

the connected one-parameter symmetry generated by \(K\) is

\[
\boxed{U(1)\cong SO(2)\cong S^1}.
\]

Thus \(U(1)\) is not the axis itself. It is the continuous orbital symmetry around the fixed relational center/axis.

## 5. Winding and chirality

In complex notation

\[
z=re^{i\phi}.
\]

For a closed orbit,

\[
z(\phi+2\pi)=z(\phi).
\]

The winding number is

\[
\boxed{
n=\frac1{2\pi}\oint d\phi\in\mathbb Z.
}
\]

Orientation reversal sends

\[
\phi\mapsto-\phi,
\qquad
n\mapsto-n,
\]

and in the complex representation

\[
e^{i\phi}\mapsto e^{-i\phi}=\overline{e^{i\phi}}.
\]

Thus chirality is an intrinsic orientation label of the orbital winding.

## 6. Root/lift algebra and the half-angle

For the \(n\)-fold covering map

\[
\pi_n:U(1)\to U(1),
\qquad
\pi_n(w)=w^n,
\]

a local lift of

\[
z=e^{i\phi}
\]

is

\[
w=e^{i\phi/n}.
\]

After one \(2\pi\) traversal of the base,

\[
w(\phi+2\pi)
=
e^{2\pi i/n}w(\phi).
\]

The lifted state closes only after \(n\) base turns:

\[
\boxed{\phi_{\rm closure}=2\pi n}.
\]

For \(n=2\),

\[
\boxed{
w=e^{i\phi/2},
\qquad
2\pi\mapsto-1,
\qquad
4\pi\mapsto+1.
}
\]

Hence the half-angle is the primitive double-cover case of the general orbital root/lift law.

### Theorem 6.1 — Primitive Double-Lift Theorem

For the primitive nontrivial cover \(\pi_2(w)=w^2\), the lifted phase is half the base phase and its closure period is doubled:

\[
\boxed{
\phi\mapsto\frac{\phi}{2},
\qquad
2\pi\mapsto4\pi.
}
\]

This is the abstract orbital precursor of the spinorial \(4\pi\) closure.

## 7. Projective two-channel realization

Let the two relational roles be represented by a nonzero complex pair

\[
(z_0,z_1)\in\mathbb C^2\setminus\{0\}.
\]

Only their relation is retained under common complex rescaling,

\[
(z_0,z_1)\sim\lambda(z_0,z_1),
\qquad
\lambda\in\mathbb C^\times.
\]

Therefore the ray space is

\[
\boxed{
(\mathbb C^2\setminus\{0\})/\mathbb C^\times
=
\mathbb{CP}^1.
}
\]

With normalized representative

\[
|z_0|^2+|z_1|^2=1,
\]

define the relational share

\[
q=\frac{|z_1|^2}{|z_0|^2+|z_1|^2}.
\]

Exchange of the channels sends

\[
q\mapsto1-q.
\]

Hence the relational zero condition is

\[
q=\frac12
\iff
|z_0|=|z_1|.
\]

For the ratio

\[
\xi=\frac{z_1}{z_0},
\]

this becomes

\[
\boxed{
|\xi|=1
\iff
q=\frac12
\iff
Z=0_R.
}
\]

Thus the unit circle in the complex ratio coordinate is the phase orbit over the equal-weight relational seam.

In the Bloch realization, this seam is the equator.

## 8. Euler--Chern--Hopf realization of the same half structure

For

\[
\mathbb{CP}^1\simeq S^2,
\]

let \(u\) denote a generator of

\[
H^2(\mathbb{CP}^1,\mathbb Z),
\qquad
\int_{\mathbb{CP}^1}u=1.
\]

The tangent bundle satisfies

\[
\boxed{
c_1(T\mathbb{CP}^1)=e(TS^2)=2u.
}
\]

The primitive Hopf line bundle has first Chern class \(\pm u\), depending on orientation/dual convention. The spin square root of the canonical bundle satisfies

\[
K_{\mathbb{CP}^1}\cong\mathcal O(-2),
\qquad
S^{\otimes2}\cong K_{\mathbb{CP}^1},
\qquad
S\cong\mathcal O(-1).
\]

Therefore, in absolute topological degree,

\[
\boxed{
|c_1(S)|=1
=
\frac12\,|c_1(T\mathbb{CP}^1)|.
}
\]

Equivalently, with compatible orientation convention, the Berry/Hopf curvature is one half of the tangent curvature normalization:

\[
\boxed{
F_{\rm Hopf}
=
\frac12 F_{\rm tangent}
}
\]

up to the sign convention of the chosen line bundle.

This is the global topological realization of the same primitive double-lift structure established abstractly in Section 6.

## 9. Critical-axis representation

Let

\[
s=\sigma+it
\]

and consider the reflection

\[
\mathcal R(s)=1-\overline{s}.
\]

Then

\[
\sigma\mapsto1-\sigma.
\]

Define the centered real coordinate

\[
\boxed{
Z_\zeta(s)=2\Re(s)-1.
}
\]

Under reflection,

\[
Z_\zeta(\mathcal R s)=-Z_\zeta(s).
\]

The fixed set is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Fix}(\mathcal R)
=
\left\{s:\Re(s)=\frac12\right\}
=
\{s:Z_\zeta(s)=0\}.
}
\]

Thus the critical line is an exact realization of the relational zero axis for this reflection.

This establishes the central/fixed axis. It does **not** by itself prove that every nontrivial zero of the Riemann zeta function lies on that axis. Such localization requires an additional theorem excluding symmetric off-axis pairs.
