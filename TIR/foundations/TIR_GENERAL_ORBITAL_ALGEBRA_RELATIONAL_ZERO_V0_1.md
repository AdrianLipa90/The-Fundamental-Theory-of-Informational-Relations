\[
\operatorname{Exist}(x)\Longrightarrow \exists R_x.

\[
\mathsf N := \neg\exists R.

\[
\operatorname{Exist}(\mathsf N)\Longrightarrow \exists R_{\mathsf N}

\[
\mathsf N\Longrightarrow\neg\exists R,

\[

## 2. Minimal dual relation and the relational midpoint

Let a nontrivial ordered relation have distinct endpoint roles \(a\neq b\). Orientation exchange is the involution

\[
\mathcal J(a)=b,\qquad \mathcal J(b)=a,\qquad \mathcal J^2=\mathrm{id}.
\]

On the affine carrier between the endpoint coordinates, the exchange map is

\[
\mathcal J(x)=a+b-x.
\]

A fixed point \(m\) satisfies

\[
m=\mathcal J(m)=a+b-m,
\]

therefore

\[
\boxed{m=\frac{a+b}{2}}.
\]

This midpoint is unique.

Normalize the endpoints by

\[
q(x)=\frac{x-a}{b-a},
\]

so that

\[
q(a)=0,\qquad q(b)=1.
\]

Then exchange acts as

\[
q\mapsto1-q
\]

and its unique fixed point is

\[
\boxed{q_*=\frac12}.
\]

Define the centered relational coordinate

\[
\boxed{Z=2q-1}.
\]

Then

\[
q=0\mapsto Z=-1,\qquad
q=\frac12\mapsto Z=0,\qquad
q=1\mapsto Z=+1,
\]

and exchange becomes

\[
\boxed{Z\mapsto-Z}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{\frac12_{[0,1]}=0_R}
\]

where \(0_R\) denotes the zero of the centered relational coordinate, not arithmetic identity with the number \(0\).

### Theorem 2.1 — Relational Zero Theorem

For every nondegenerate affine two-endpoint relation with endpoint exchange involution, the unique exchange-invariant point is the midpoint. Under normalization to \([0,1]\), that point has coordinate \(1/2\); under centered relational coordinates it is \(0_R\).
